# 🎯 01 · Intent: Our Problem, Before Any AI

📜 **Disclosure pages:** [0 Start](00-start-here%20%28disclosure%29.md) · [1 Intent](01-intent.md) · [2 Research](02-research-brief.md) · [3 Sources](03-source-verification-log.md) · [4 AI use + prompts](04-ai-use-log.md) · [5 Outputs](05-outputs.md) · [6 Decisions](06-decision-note.md) · [7 Disclosure](07-disclosure.md)

## ❓ Our problem
1. Geometry students write **proofs**. A proof is a list of steps. Each step needs a **reason**.
2. Students **get stuck**. They cannot see **which step** is weak.
3. AI tools can help. But open AI tools can **give the answer**. Then students **learn less**.

**Our question:** how can an AI help with **1 weak step**, and **not** do the work?

## 📥 What we received
1. 📄 The **DIGI 230 brief**: map 1 client course. Find 1 responsible AI use.
2. 📚 The **MATH 140** catalog description and syllabus (Weeks 1–10).
3. 🗂️ The **class templates**: the course map, the stakeholder matrix, the M2 proposal, and the build pack.

## 🔢 Our steps before AI
1. We picked a client course. **First SUST 720. Then MATH 140** (Sept 29).
2. We named the people: students, the teacher, partners, tutors, and kids without computers.
3. We marked **where AI must stay out**.

## 💥 Where it breaks down
- The student who needs help is the **worst at spotting a wrong hint**.
- A hint that is **too exact** is the answer.
- Some kids have **no computer**.

## ✅ What a good result looks like
1. **1 hint** about **1 step**. No answers.
2. A **human check** on every unsure hint.
3. A **no-AI way** that counts the same.
4. Words that an **8-year-old** can read.

## 🚫 What AI must not decide
1. **Grades.**
2. If a proof is **correct**.
3. **Cheating.**
4. **Our rules.** We decide them. The AI follows them.

## 🍓 Material we used
**Only made-up proofs and hints.** No real student work. No student data.
