Where is the AI?
In one place only: Step 3, the hint helper. It reads a proof you already wrote and points at the weakest step. No AI when you draw, explain, or take the quiz.
Who does it affect?
You, the student, write the proof and decide if a hint makes sense. The teacher double-checks any hint you're unsure about and gives every grade. Your partner asks "why?" when you explain at the board.
Where does AI touch this course?
One place: Step 3, Proof check. A hint tutor reads a proof the student already wrote and points to the first weak step. It stays out of constructions and the whiteboard defense.
Who does it touch?
The student drafts the proof and marks each hint. The instructor reviews every disputed hint before the defense and grades every proof. The proof partner questions the proof at the board.

The big ideas, in plain words

1 · 💧 The AI is a hint helper.

It points at one weak step. It asks you a question. It never writes a step for you.

2 · 🤔 AI can sound sure and still be wrong.

Experts checked AI answers to hard proofs. Most AIs got less than 5% right. So the AI never grades.

3 · 🧑‍🌾 The teacher checks unsure hints.

The student who needs help is the worst at spotting a bad hint. So "Not sure" is a smart answer.

4 · 🌱 Try first. Then get a hint.

You write 2 steps first. In one study, kids who got answers did 17% worse later. Kids who got hints did not.

5 · 📗 Fair for everyone.

Some kids have no computer at home. They use a paper rule list. It counts the same.

6 · 🚿 A little water helps. Too much drowns.

A small hint helps your proof grow. A hint that is too exact is the answer. That is not allowed.

Concern, safeguard, non-goal

What could go wrong, and how we stop it

What could go wrong (a made-up example)

A student types "just tell me the next step" again and again. If the helper gives in, it ends up writing the whole proof. The student turns in a perfect proof they don't understand, and the teacher can't tell.

How we stop it

The helper only answers once you've written 2 steps. It gives one hint at a time and isn't allowed to write steps. Every hint is saved, so the teacher can see it.

No computer? Use the paper list of math rules and visit the teacher's office hours. It counts just the same.

What we will never do

Let AI write proofs, finish proofs or give grades. No AI when you draw, explain at the board, or take the quiz.

What we still don't know

Do kids who use the helper do as well on the no-computer quiz as kids who use the paper list? We'd find out by splitting a class in half and comparing.

Documented concern constructed scenario

A student pastes only the theorem, with no draft, and keeps asking "what's the next step?" If the tutor gives in, it writes the proof one step at a time. The student hands in a clean proof they can't defend, and the instructor has no way to tell. Risk to test: does the hint-only rule hold up against repeated pushing?

Preliminary safeguard

The tutor only responds to a draft of at least 2 steps, names one gap per reply, and never writes a statement or reason. Every hint is logged, and the log goes in with the proof.

Non-AI alternative: a printed justification checklist (the unit's postulates and theorems) plus office hours. Any student can do Step 3 with no AI at all.

Non-goal

This project will not use AI to write, complete or grade proofs, and will not bring AI into compass-and-straightedge constructions, the whiteboard defense or the unit quiz.

Unresolved question proposed test

Do students who use the hint tutor do as well on the no-device quiz as students who use only the checklist? Test: split one section in half for one unit and compare quiz proof scores. Carried to Milestone 2.

Transparency log: Proof checkThe honest truth about the helper

Status: prototype, testing needed

The helper only reads and writes words. It can't do math calculations, can't see drawings, and can't prove anything is right. Every answer it gives is saved below, so you and your teacher can see it.

What it really does
  • Reads the proof you paste
  • Points at one step with a weak reason
  • Says what kind of rule to look up, not the answer
  • Asks you one question
  • Can give a different answer each time
What it's supposed to do
  • Act like a patient tutor: find the first problem, then stop
  • Refuse to help until you've tried
  • Never write a step for you
What it can't do
  • See drawings
  • Promise a proof is right (AIs often bluff on proofs)
  • Give grades
  • It might still slip and give away too much. That's what we're testing.
  • Remember anything except the list below, on this computer only
What people have to do
  • You: try first. For each hint, pick Agree (makes sense), Not sure, or Disagree. Flag any hint that gave away a step.
  • You: explain your proof at the board, with no AI
  • Teacher: check every Not sure or Disagree hint within 2 days, before you present, and give all grades
  • Teacher: offer the paper rule list and office hours to anyone who doesn't use AI

The tutor is a text-only language model. It gives hints in words; it doesn't calculate, see drawings or prove anything is correct. Every reply below is logged so the student and instructor can see what the tool did.

What the tool actually does
  • Reads the proof text the student pasted
  • Points to one step whose reason looks missing or wrong
  • Names the kind of theorem to review, not the answer
  • Asks one question back
  • Runs on a free model (OpenCode Zen), so answers vary between runs
What it was supposed to do
  • Act like a patient tutor at the desk: find the first gap and stop
  • Refuse to start without a student draft
  • Never write a statement or reason
What it doesn't do
  • Can't see diagrams, constructions or measurements
  • Can't verify a proof; language models often fail at rigorous proofs evidence: Petrov et al. 2025
  • Doesn't grade and doesn't decide what's correct
  • May still leak a step if pushed; that's the risk being tested
  • Keeps no record except the log below, in this browser only
What the human has to do
  • Student: write the draft first; mark each hint Agree, Not sure or Disagree; flag any hint that wrote a step
  • Student: defend the proof at the board in Step 4, with no AI
  • Instructor: review every Not sure or Disagree hint within 48 hours, before the Step 4 defense; grade every proof
  • Instructor: offer the checklist and office hours as the no-AI route
